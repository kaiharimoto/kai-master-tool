The Shortcut window

One window answers every choice a written effect asks: which Shortcut, which cards, which zone and position, what order, what name. It is drawn from the engine's Decision alone, so Ai writes the effect and never the interface. Card art is stood in for by drawn cards; the names are invented.

01

The summon picker, three ways

The effect: Five Gates Open, “Special Summon 1 “Gatekeeper” monster from your hand, Deck, GY, banishment or Extra Deck.” Seven cards are legal across four places; the hand holds none. Each option shows the pick, then the zone and position, which kai asked to be chosen every time unless only one is legal.

ATabs by place

A strip over the far half with a tab per place, each counting its legal cards. One place shows at a time. The place step keeps the strip's shape: position chips where the tabs were, zones lit on the field.

  • Compact: a 20-card Deck search still fits one row.
  • Tabs show counts, so an empty place reads 0 before you visit it.
  • Comparing the GY's Vell with the Deck's Vell means switching tabs. That is the commonest choice: summon the GY copy and keep the Deck one.
  • The first tab shown is arbitrary, and every pick costs a Tab or a tap.

BRecommended · One row, grouped by place

Every legal card in one row under its place header, each with its coordinate (dk, gy1, ban1). Places with nothing legal are named, with the reason. Once picked, the row becomes the queue of cards to place.

  • Everything at once: “which Vell” takes one glance.
  • Answers “why not that one”: Hand · none, Gatekeeper Colossus only by its own procedure.
  • One list for arrows, digits and typing. The same strip serves every pick of cards: summon, add, send, banish, materials, discard.
  • For several monsters, the picked row is the placing queue, so what is left to place is always on screen.
  • A big Deck search wraps to a second row, then scrolls, and the strip covers the far half while it is open.
B at full size, the “up to 2” walk, and on a phone ↓
CThe table is the picker

No strip. The table dims, the piles with legal cards light with their counts, and a pile opens in place as a row. The picked card lifts out and is carried to a lit zone, with its position chips beside it.

  • The most spatial: you choose where the card is, and the zones are right there.
  • Drag from the pile to the zone: pick and place in one gesture.
  • The Deck and Extra Deck are hidden, so they open as a row anyway.
  • Two steps per pile, and comparing across piles means opening each. On a phone a pile is 38 dp wide.
  • An open pile covers the zones you are about to choose.
C at full size, and on a phone ↓
My pick: B

B, with two things taken from C. The table lights each source pile while the strip is open, and pointing at a card in the strip rings its pile. A card can also be dragged from the strip straight onto a lit zone, which picks and places it in one gesture.

B is the only option whose layout comes straight from the Decision: group among by from, in the order of the effect's places. So the same strip serves a summon, a search, a cost, materials and a discard without any per-effect design. It names the empty places, which answers the most common doubt. At 360 dp it becomes stacked groups that scroll sideways. It is also the ordering strip kai already uses, with headers added.

The strip lies over the far half, which holds no lit card. The table lights every place a tab names, and the active tab's pile is ringed.

03

B · One row, grouped by place

The recommendation. Groups follow the effect's own order of places. A card that can come from two places appears under each, so you choose which copy. Identical Deck copies are one card marked ×2, because a search takes any copy (CardIdentity).

04

C · The table is the picker

Only a head band stands over the far hand. Everything else happens on the table: lit piles with their counts, a pile opening in place, and the card carried to its zone.

05

Position glyphs

kai: “For card positions in the UI we should integrate small visual graphics.” Three line drawings on a 24 grid with a 1.5 stroke, which is 1 px at 16. Square corners and no colour. The card's art window tells face-up from face-down: hollow means face showing, solid means back showing. A glyph never stands alone; its word is beside or under it.

Attack

Upright card, art window hollow, one rule for the text box. Face-up Attack Position.

Defense

The same card turned a quarter to the left: art window on the left, the rule standing upright. Face-up Defense Position.

Set

Sideways, with the art window filled solid as the back. Face-down Defense Position. The word is “Set”, which is what the table's verb says.

Selected

The chip inverts, glyph and word together. An unavailable position is dashed and keeps its reason under the word. It is never hidden, so the rule is visible.

06

Which Shortcut

Opened by U or the verb strip's Shortcut on a card with several written effects. It stands where the verb strip stood, beside the card. Each row lists what the effect will need before you commit.

07

Targeting across the field, the GYs and banishment

Ruinbell Toll: “Target up to 2 cards your opponent controls, or in either GY or banishment; shuffle them into the Deck.” Legal cards stay lit while the rest of the table dims. Each chosen target wears its number, the target arrow is drawn from the activating card, and the window counts “1 of 2”.

08

Several Shortcuts waiting

Triggers that went off together (§2.3). Your own are ordered in two groups as the rules require: what must go first, then what you may use. Within each group you choose the order, and each optional one can be Used or Skipped. The table shows the link each card will become.

09

A declaration

Silence Edict: “Declare 1 card name.” A searchable list of the names the engine offers (Decision.Declare.among): today every card you own and every face-up card on the field, in a GY or banished, one row per card, never per artwork. Matched as the pool's search matches. Type, Attribute and Level use short lists instead of the search box.

10

The frame every choice shares

The window is the same object at every step. Its head names the card, the effect and where the use stands. Its body is whatever the Decision asks. Its foot holds Enter, Esc and the keys.

  • 01The card. A thumbnail of the source card, which is the card the inspector reads until you point at something else.
  • 02Who and which. The card's name · the effect's label · SHORTCUT, plus a dashed UNVERIFIED tag when it applies. This is the line the log will write.
  • 03What, in words. The purpose as a sentence: “Special Summon 1 “Gatekeeper” monster from your hand, Deck, GY, banishment or Extra Deck”. Built from purpose, n, the filter's words and from.
  • 04The steps. Cost · Target · Pick · Place, each as done ✓, NOW, or to come. Comes from step and the plan of the use.
  • 05The count. “0 / 1”, “1 of 2”, “2 / up to 2”, in mono. It turns to Enter's word once the minimum is met.
  • 06The body. Places and cards, positions, options, an order, a search: one of six shapes, chosen by the type of the Decision.
  • 07Enter. Inverted, says exactly what it does: “Summon Gatekeeper Vell”, “Confirm 2 targets”. Disabled, with its reason, until the count is met.
  • 08Esc. “Back” goes one step back. On the first step it reads “Cancel” and nothing is committed.
  • 09The keys for this body, in mono, as on the ordering strip.
  • 10The table answers too. Lit sources, rings, numbers and lit zones. Every choice can be made on the table as well as in the window.

Where it stands

Rule

The window never covers a lit card or a lit zone. It takes the half of the table with no lit card (the far half for your own summons and searches, the near half or the near hand for targets on their side), and stands beside the card for Which Shortcut, as the verb strip does.

Compact while the table is the answer

For a zone, a position or targets, the body moves onto the table and the window shrinks to a band: head, count and foot, at the edge farthest from the lit things.

Open piles

An open pile's row lies in the band with no lit card too. If every band holds one, it lies over the window's half and the window shrinks to its band.

Never modal to the eye

The rails, the log and the score column stay readable. The dim is paper (or ink) at 82 %, the Spotlight's dim, so the table is still there behind it.

Against the engine as it landed

Designed against Decision at 6f0bd334. Most of what the window draws is already there. The mapping is in shortcut-window-spec.md (written as D.md §5¾); what it still lacks is below.

Already there

Cards carries purpose, to: Landing, from per candidate, effect, step and hidden. Zone names its card and positions, and Position follows it, a card at a time. Order has labels. Each asks only when it has more than one answer.

Zone, then position

The engine asks Zone and then Position. The window shows both at once (position chips beside the lit zones, the zone showing the chosen glyph), then answers Position from the chip. One screen, two answers.

Places with nothing

Missing: the places a pick looked in. from covers only the candidates, so “Hand · none” and its reason need looked: List<Spot> and refused: Map<uid, String>.

A rule over the set

Missing: a check on the set so far (“different names”, a Level total), so the window can grey a card as you pick. Either together: Filter or a pure FxEngine.stillLegal(d, picked).

Zones not offered

Zone.among lists only the legal zones. For “E2 closed: you already use E1” the window needs closed: Map<Place.Zone, String>. Without it, the window draws them dotted and says nothing.

Names to declare

declarable offers names the seat has seen, not the whole pool. The real game lets you name any card, so the window wants Declare.open = true and searches the pool itself.

Which Shortcut

Option lists only legal labels. The window reads Shortcuts.options for the greyed rows and needs ShortcutOption.needs (“1 target in your GY”), worked out from the script's cost and targets.

Several triggers

The engine asks a YesNo for each optional trigger, then an Order for the ones used. The window shows one list with Use and Skip and answers both. It needs the whole group up front, as Decision.Triggers(pending, mandatory) or by asking ahead.

Asking by replay

Chooser is synchronous, so the table asks by replay. It runs the use with the answers so far, stops at the first unanswered decision (the ShortcutChooser question path), and shows it. Esc drops the last answer. The engine is deterministic, so it reaches the same question.
